How to Choose Property for Accommodation Business

Property selection is the foundation of a successful accommodation business. The right property can make operations smooth and profitable, while the wrong choice can lead to endless challenges. This guide covers the key factors to evaluate when selecting a property for accommodation use in Japan.

Zoning and Legal Restrictions

Not all properties can legally be used for accommodation. Japan's zoning system restricts accommodation use to certain zones, and the specific regulations vary by ward and license type. Before considering any property, verify that its zoning classification permits hotel or private lodging use. Properties in exclusive residential zones may have severe restrictions or outright prohibitions. A professional zoning check is essential before making an offer.

Building Type and Condition

The building's structure, age, and condition directly impact both your ability to obtain permits and your renovation costs. Reinforced concrete (RC) buildings generally offer better soundproofing, making them preferable for accommodation. Wooden buildings may require additional fire safety measures. Building code compliance — including earthquake resistance, fire protection, and accessibility — must be verified. Older buildings may require significant investment to meet current standards.

Profitability Analysis

Beyond the purchase price, evaluate potential revenue based on location, room count, and target ADR. Factor in renovation costs, permit fees, ongoing operational expenses (utilities, cleaning, management, OTA commissions), and property taxes. Create realistic financial projections with conservative occupancy assumptions. Properties that look cheap upfront may have hidden costs that erode margins, while slightly more expensive properties in prime locations may offer better returns.

Location and Accessibility

Proximity to train stations, tourist attractions, restaurants, and convenience stores significantly affects guest satisfaction and booking rates. Evaluate the neighborhood's safety, noise levels, and appeal to your target guest segment. Also consider the practical aspects for operations — can cleaners and suppliers access the property easily? Is there parking if needed? A thorough location assessment will prevent many operational issues later.
